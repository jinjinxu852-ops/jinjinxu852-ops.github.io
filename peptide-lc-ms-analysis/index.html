LC-MS Analysis

HPLC tells you how many species are in the sample; LC-MS tells you what each of them is. The combination is the right tool whenever an unexplained peak matters.

What LC-MS does

The sample passes through a reversed-phase column exactly as in analytical HPLC, but instead of ending at a UV detector the eluent flows into a mass spectrometer, usually through an electrospray source. The instrument records a mass spectrum continuously, so every point in the chromatogram carries mass information.

The practical consequence: each peak can be identified, not merely counted. An impurity at 8.2 minutes is no longer “an unknown peak” but a species of defined molecular weight, which usually explains itself.

Where it earns its cost

Identifying impurities. A by-product 16 Da heavier than the target is an oxidation product; one lighter by the mass of a residue is a deletion sequence. Knowing which it is tells you whether the material is usable and how to prevent the problem next time.

Modified and labelled peptides. Phosphopeptides with several sites, and fluorescently labelled peptides where the dye is an isomeric mixture, give complex chromatograms. LC-MS confirms each peak carries the intended modification.

Co-eluting species. When two species have nearly the same hydrophobicity, UV detection shows one peak. The mass trace across that peak reveals both.

Stability studies. Repeat analysis of a peptide held under defined conditions shows which degradation products appear and how fast, so you can set a realistic expiry for your stock.

Peptides from other sources. When a peptide underperforms and the supplier’s certificate looks fine, LC-MS is usually the test that finds the discrepancy.

What you receive

  • Total ion chromatogram and UV trace from the same run
  • Mass spectrum for each significant peak
  • Table of observed masses with proposed assignments
  • Method details: column, gradient, ionisation mode and mass range

Frequently asked questions

When should I choose LC-MS over separate HPLC and MS?

When you need to know which mass belongs to which peak. Standalone mass spectrometry of a mixture gives all the masses at once without saying which species they came from. LC-MS links each one to its retention time, which is what identifying an impurity requires.

Can LC-MS tell me where a phosphate group is?

LC-MS separates positional isomers chromatographically and confirms that each carries the phosphate, which is often enough. Pinpointing the exact residue requires fragmentation (MS/MS); tell us if your project needs site localisation and we will confirm what we can provide.

Will LC-MS detect an impurity that HPLC missed?

Often yes. Species that co-elute with the main peak are invisible in a UV trace but appear as a separate mass in the mass spectrum across that peak.

How much sample is needed?

LC-MS is sensitive and needs far less material than amino acid analysis — typically well under 1 mg is ample. Tell us what you can spare.
